Episode 2: The Totally Grossed-Out Maid

Lilya was a guard attendant of the Asura rear palace.

A guard attendant is an attendant who also has the qualities of a royal guard.

Ordinarily she does an attendant's work, but in an emergency she takes up the sword and protects her liege.

Lilya was faithful to her duties, and she handled her work as an attendant without a slip.

However, as a swordswoman she had nothing more than dime-a-dozen talent.

Therefore, fighting an assassin who was after the newborn princess, she was caught off guard and ended up taking a dagger in the leg.

The dagger had been coated with poison. The kind of poison used to try to kill royalty.

A nasty poison, with no detoxification magic that could undo it.

The wound was immediately closed with healing magic, and thanks to the doctor's attempts to counteract the poison her life was spared, but aftereffects remained.

They did not get in the way of daily life, but she could no longer run at full speed, or step in sharply.

Lilya's life as a swordswoman came to an end that day.

The royal palace dismissed Lilya without a second thought.

It was nothing unusual. Lilya accepted it, too.

Lose your usefulness, lose your job. Only natural.

She did not receive even enough money to live on for the time being, but the mere fact that she had not been secretly executed for having served in the rear palace was, she had to tell herself, a stroke of luck.

Lilya left the royal capital.

The mastermind behind the attempt on the princess's life had not yet been found.

Lilya, who knew the floor plan of the rear palace, understood full well that she herself might be targeted.

Or perhaps the royal palace was letting Lilya swim free, to fish out the mastermind.

Back then, she had wondered how she, from a family of no great standing, had gotten into the rear palace, but looking back now, maybe what they had wanted was disposable maids.

In any case, for her own protection as well, she needed to get as far from the royal capital as she could.

Even if the royal palace had released her into the water as bait, as long as she had been given no orders, nothing bound her.

Nor did she feel any need to play the loyal servant.

Changing from one stagecoach to the next, Lilya came to the Fittoa Region, a frontier where a vast farming country stretched on and on.

Apart from Roa, the fortress city where the lord lived, it was a peaceful place with wheat fields stretching as far as the eye could see.

Lilya decided to look for work there.

That said, with her injured leg, she could not do rough work.

She might be able to teach swordsmanship, at least, but if possible, she wanted to be hired as an attendant.

Because that paid better.

Out here on the frontier there were plenty of people who could use a sword, and people who could teach it, but educated attendants who could do a household's work perfectly were few.

Low supply, high wages.

But, being hired as an attendant to the lord of Fittoa, or to an upper noble of similar standing, was dangerous.

People like that, naturally, had connections to the royal capital as well.

If it got out that she had been a guard attendant attached to the rear palace, she might be used as a political card.

No thanks. Absolutely not.

Nearly dying once was plenty. Never again.

Her apologies to the princess, but the royal family could fight over the succession on their own, somewhere far away where she never had to hear about it.

That said, anywhere the pay was too low, she could not even send money home to her family.

Terms that could satisfy both, pay and safety, were not easy to find.

After a month of making the rounds from place to place, one posting caught her eye.

In Buena Village, the Fittoa Region: a lower-rank knight seeking an attendant.

Preference given to those with child-rearing experience and a midwife's knowledge, it said.

Buena Village was a small village on the edge of the Fittoa Region.

Rural as rural gets: the back end of the boonies.

It was an inconvenient place, but a location like that was exactly what she had been looking for.

Besides, the terms were so good you would never think the employer was a lower-rank knight.

Above all, she recognized the name of the one hiring.

'Paulo Greyrat'

He was Lilya's junior at the dojo.

One day, out of nowhere, he had come tumbling into the dojo where Lilya was learning the sword: a noble family's good-for-nothing brat.

Word was he had fought with his father and been disowned, or some such, and he started learning the sword while crashing at the dojo.

Though the school was different, he had also been taught swordsmanship at home, and in no time at all he overtook Lilya.

It had galled Lilya, but by now she had made her peace with it: she simply had not had the talent.

Paulo, overflowing with talent, one day caused some trouble and ran off from the dojo.

Leaving Lilya just one line: "I'm going to be an adventurer."

The man had been a walking storm.

Seven years or so since they had parted, she supposed.

To think that the Paulo of back then had actually become a knight, and even gotten married, of all things...

She did not know what kind of stormy, up-and-down life he had led, but the Paulo in Lilya's memory was by no means a bad guy.

If she told him she was in trouble, he would probably help her.

If that did not work, she would dredge up the past.

She had a few stories on hand that would make good bargaining chips.

Such were Lilya's calculations as she made her way to Buena Village.

Paulo welcomed Lilya warmly.

The lady of the house, Zenith, was due to give birth soon, and it seemed he had been getting anxious.

Lilya had had every kind of knowledge and skill drilled into her in preparation for the princess's birth and upbringing, and as an acquaintance of known origin, her background checked out, too.

She was welcomed with open arms.

He even said he would pay her more than planned, so for Lilya too, it was a dream come true.

★　★　★

The child was born.

It was not a difficult birth, not in the least: a delivery exactly like the ones she had practiced in the rear palace.

Nothing went wrong. It went smoothly.

And yet, the newborn did not cry.

Lilya broke into a cold sweat.

Right after the birth she had suctioned its nose and mouth and drawn out the amniotic fluid, but the baby only looked up with an expressionless face and did not make a single sound.

Could it be stillborn? That was how blank its face was.

When she touched it, it was warm, and it had a pulse. It was breathing, too.

But it did not cry.

Something she had heard from a senior guard attendant crossed Lilya's mind.

A baby that does not cry right after birth often has something wrong with it.

Surely not, she thought, and in the very next moment,

"Aah, uwaah."

The baby looked her way and, with a vacant expression, murmured something.

Hearing that, Lilya was relieved.

She had no grounds at all, but somehow, it seemed it would be all right.

★　★　★

The child was named Rudeus.

He was an eerie child. He never cried at all, and never fussed. Maybe he was sickly, but at least he was low-maintenance.

She could only afford to think that way at the very start.

Once Rudeus could crawl, he went anywhere and everywhere in the house.

Anywhere in the house, and that meant anywhere. The kitchen, the back door, the storeroom, the cleaning closet, inside the fireplace... and so on.

How he climbed up, no one knew, but there were even times he got all the way up to the second floor.

In any case, the moment she took her eyes off him, he was gone.

Yet for some reason, he was always found inside the house.

Rudeus never once went outside the house.

There were times he looked outside through the window, but perhaps the outside was still too scary.

Lilya's instinctive fear of this baby: when had it begun?

Was it when she took her eyes off him, he disappeared, and she went looking and found him?

Most of the time, Rudeus was smiling.

One time staring at vegetables in the kitchen, one time staring at the flame wavering on a candle in its candlestick, and yet another time staring at unwashed underwear.

Rudeus would mumble something under his breath, then break into a creepy grin and laugh.

—It was a smile that stirred a gut-level revulsion.

While Lilya served in the rear palace, her duties had taken her to the royal palace a number of times, and it closely resembled the smile of a minister she had met there.

It looked just like the smile he wore as he ogled Lilya's chest, his bald head glistening, his big fat gut jiggling. That was what it looked like, the smile on a newborn baby.

Scariest of all was when she picked Rudeus up.

Rudeus would flare his nostrils, lift the corners of his mouth, breathe hard through his nose, and press his face into her chest.

And then, his throat twitching, he would laugh in a strange voice, somewhere halfway between "Fuheh" and "Ohoh."

In that instant, a hair-raising chill took hold of her whole body.

A chill so bad it gave her the reflexive urge to slam the baby in her arms down onto the floor.

Not one shred of a baby's cuteness in it. This smile was loathsome, pure and simple.

The very same smile as that minister, the one rumored to be buying up young slave girls by the dozen.

And here was a newborn baby wearing it.

It was unpleasant beyond all comparison; she even felt herself in danger, from a baby.

Lilya considered it.

Something was wrong with this baby. Maybe some evil Thing had possessed him. Or maybe he was cursed.

Once the idea struck her, Lilya could not sit still.

She ran to the general store and, spending what little money she had, purchased what she needed.

When the Greyrat household had fallen asleep, she performed a ward against evil handed down in her homeland.

Without Paulo's permission, of course.

The next day, picking Rudeus up, Lilya realized.

That it had been useless.

He was as creepy as ever. A baby making a face like that was eerie enough all on its own.

Zenith too had said things like, "That boy licks, you know, when I nurse him..."

Unthinkable, she thought.

Paulo was a skirt-chaser with zero restraint, too, but even he was not this creepy.

Even if it ran in the family, this was going too far.

Lilya remembered. Ah, come to think of it, she had heard a story like this in the rear palace.

'Once, there was an incident in which a prince of Asura went about the rear palace on all fours, night after night. The prince was possessed by a devil. If an attendant, not knowing this, carelessly picked the prince up, the prince would take the knife he had hidden behind his back and kill her with a single thrust to the heart.'

How horrifying.

Rudeus was one of Those.

No doubt about it. He was absolutely that kind of devil.

For now he was behaving himself, but someday he would awaken, and when the whole house was asleep, one, then another...

Oh... she had jumped the gun. She had so obviously jumped the gun. She never should have taken a job at a place like this.

One of these days he would come for her, she was sure of it.

...Lilya was the type who took superstitions dead seriously.

★　★　★

For the first year or so, she was frightened like that.

But since when? Rudeus's behavior, once impossible to predict, fell into a pattern.

He no longer popped up here, there and everywhere, and took to shutting himself up in Paulo's study, in a corner of the second floor.

It was called a study, but it was a plain room with only a few books in it.

Rudeus would shut himself up in there and not come out. When she peeked in, he was gazing at a book and muttering something.

They were not meaningful words.

They couldn't be. At least, it was not a language commonly used on the Central Continent.

He was still too young to be talking. And of course, no one had taught him letters.

So the baby was just looking at the books and making random sounds.

Anything else would make no sense.

But no matter what she did, to Lilya it could not help sounding like a string of words that meant something.

It could not help looking to her as if Rudeus understood what was in the books.

Frightening... So Lilya thought, watching Rudeus through the gap in the door.

Yet strangely, she felt no revulsion.

Come to think of it, since he had begun shutting himself up in the study, the eeriness and creepiness she could not put a name to had gradually died down.

He still smiled creepily now and then, but picking him up no longer made her feel uncomfortable.

He did not bury his face in her chest, and his breathing did not get heavy.

Why had she ever thought this child was loathsome?

Lately, if anything, she had come to sense in him a sincerity and diligence that made her feel she must not disturb him.

When she talked it over with Zenith, it seemed Zenith felt the same way.

Since then, she had come to think, might it not be better to leave him alone?

She thought it an abnormal way to feel.

Leaving a newborn to its own devices was no way for a human being to behave.

But lately, the light of intellect had come into Rudeus's eyes.

Into eyes in which, until a few months ago, she could sense nothing but lecherous idiocy.

A firm will, and an intellect that all but shone.

What should she do? For Lilya, who had knowledge but little experience, it was hard to judge.

There is no right answer in raising children: had the one who said that been a senior guard attendant, or her mother back home?

At least for now he was not creepy, and he did not make her uncomfortable. No shudder of loathing ran through her.

Then there was no need to disturb him and turn him back to how he had been.

—Leave him be.

That was the judgment Lilya finally reached.
