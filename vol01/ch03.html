Episode 3: Magic Textbook

About two years had gone by since I was reincarnated.

My legs and back had gotten sturdier, and I'd achieved unassisted bipedal locomotion.

I was also getting to where I could speak this world's language.

★　★　★

Having decided to live seriously, I thought about what to do first.

What had I needed when I was alive?

Study, exercise, skills.

There's not much a baby can do. At most, burying my face in a chest whenever I get picked up.

When I do that to the maid, she makes a blatantly disgusted face.

That maid must hate kids, no doubt about it.

Having decided exercise could wait a little longer, I started reading the books in the house so I could learn the letters.

Language study is important.

The Japanese have close to 100% literacy in their own language, but plenty of them are bad at English, and plenty of them balk when it comes to going abroad. To the point that having mastered a foreign language counts as a skill in itself. So I made learning this world's letters my first task.

The house had all of five books.

Are books expensive in this world, or are Paulo and Zenith not readers?

Probably both. Unbelievable, for a man who owned a library of several thousand volumes.

Granted, every last one was a light novel.

Only five books, but that was plenty for learning to read.

This world's language was close to Japanese, so I was able to pick it up quickly.

The shapes of the letters are completely different, but the grammar-type stuff went right in.

All I had to do was memorize the words. It also helped a lot that I'd learned the spoken language first.

My father had read the books aloud to me a number of times, so I was able to learn the words smoothly.

This body's good memory might have something to do with it too.

Once you can read the letters, the books are fun.

I used to think I would never in my whole life find studying fun, but when I really think it over, it's like memorizing info for an online game. No way that's not fun.

That said, does that father of mine think an infant can understand what's in a book?

It worked out because it was me, but with a normal toddler it'd be a total outrage. The kid would scream bloody murder.

The books in the house were the following five.

'Walking the World'

A guidebook with the names and features of the countries of the world.

'The Monsters of Fittoa: Habits and Weaknesses'

The habits of the monsters that appear in the region called Fittoa, and how to deal with them.

'Magic Textbook'

A textbook for mages, covering attack magic from Beginner through Advanced.

'The Legend of Pergius'

A summoning mage named Pergius fights a demon god together with his companions and saves the world: a fairy tale of good rewarded and evil punished.

'The Three Swordsmen and the Labyrinth'

An action-adventure in which three genius swordsmen of different schools meet and delve into a deep labyrinth.

The last two battle novels aside, the other three taught me a lot.

The Magic Textbook is especially interesting.

For me, a man from a world without magic, the descriptions of magic are truly fascinating.

Reading on, I learned a few basic things.

1. First, broadly divided, there are only three kinds of magic.

'Attack magic'—attacks the other person.

'Healing magic'—heals the other person.

'Summoning magic'—calls something forth.

These three. Exactly what they sound like.

You'd think it could do all sorts of other things, but according to the textbook, magic was born and raised in the middle of fighting, so apparently it isn't used much in places that have nothing to do with fighting or hunting.

2. Using magic requires mana.

Put the other way, it seems anyone can use it, as long as they have mana.

There are two ways of using mana.

'Using the mana inside your own body'

'Drawing it out of a substance charged with mana and using that'

One or the other.

I can't find a good comparison, but the former is probably something like generating your own power at home, and the latter like a battery.

Long ago, people apparently used magic with only the mana inside their own bodies, but as the generations went on, magic was researched and grew more difficult, and the mana it consumed increased explosively along with it.

That was fine for people with a lot of mana, but people with little mana couldn't use any decent magic.

So the mages of old came up with the method of sucking mana out of things other than themselves and putting it toward their magic.

3. There are two methods of activating magic.

'Incantation'

'Magic circle'

No need for a detailed explanation, I'd think. You either activate the magic by saying it out loud, or activate the magic by drawing a magic circle.

Long ago, the magic circle was apparently the main force, but now incantation is the mainstream.

The reason being, long ago even the simplest incantation apparently took around one to two minutes.

There was no way you could use it in combat.

A magic circle, on the other hand, once drawn, could be used over and over a number of times.

Incantation became the mainstream because a certain mage succeeded in drastically shortening incantations.

The simplest ones were shortened to around five seconds, and attack magic came to be used by incantation only.

Granted, summoning magic, which isn't needed to take effect immediately and requires complex spell formulas, apparently still mainly uses magic circles.

4. An individual's mana is more or less fixed from birth.

In a normal RPG, your MP goes up every time you level up.

But in this world, apparently it doesn't.

Apparently almost everyone is the Warrior class. Since it says "almost," it seems it does vary somewhat, but...

What about me, I wonder.

The Magic Textbook says the amount of mana is hereditary.

For what it's worth, my mother seems to be able to use healing magic, so maybe I'm allowed to hope for a decent amount?

I'm nervous. My parents may be talented, but my own genes look like the kind that won't do a lick of work.

★　★　★

For now, I decided to try using the simplest magic.

Basically, the Magic Textbook gave both the magic circle and the incantation, but incantation is apparently the mainstream, and I had nothing to draw a magic circle with anyway, so I decided to practice that way.

As a spell gets bigger in scale, the incantation gets longer, and apparently you have to use a magic circle alongside it and so on, but at first I should be fine.

Incidentally, a skilled mage can apparently use magic even without an incantation.

Chantless casting, shortened incantation, that kind of thing.

But why would getting skilled let you use it without an incantation?

If total mana doesn't change, then leveling up wouldn't make your MP go up either.

Then again, maybe your MP cost goes down as your proficiency goes up?

No, even if the MP cost did go down, that's no reason for there to be fewer steps, is it.

...Whatever. For now, let's just try it.

With the Magic Textbook in one hand, I thrust my right hand forward and read the words aloud.

"Where you seek, may there be the great blessing of water; let the flow of a cool, clear brook be here and now. 'Water Ball'"

There was a sensation like blood gathering in my right hand.

As if that blood were being pushed out, a fist-sized Water Ball formed at the tip of my right hand.

"Whoa!!"

So I marveled, and the very next instant, the Water Ball fell with a splash and wet the floor.

The textbook said it was a spell that sends a bullet of water flying, but it fell right there.

Maybe the magic doesn't last once your concentration breaks.

Concentrate, concentrate...

It's the feeling of gathering blood into my right hand. Like this, like this, like so... yeah.

I readied my right hand again and, recalling the sensation from before, pictured it in my head.

I don't know how much total mana I have, but I'd better assume I can't use it all that many times.

I'll concentrate as if every single practice attempt has to succeed.

First picture it in my head, repeat it over and over in my head, and only then actually try it.

If I stumble, picture that part in my head again. Until it succeeds perfectly inside my brain.

When I was alive, that's what I did when I practiced combos in fighting games.

Thanks to that, I almost never dropped a combo, even in matches.

So this way of practicing isn't wrong... is what I'd like to think.

"Hhhh... hooo..."

One deep breath.

I built up strength, as if sending blood to my right hand from the tips of my feet and the top of my head.

And then, with a feeling like spitting it out of my palm with a pop...

Carefully, carefully, in time with my heartbeat, little by little. Little by little...

Water, water, water, Water Ball, bullet of water, ball of water, polka dots, polka-dot panties...

Impure thoughts crept in. Once more.

Squeeze it together, wring it out, water water water water...

"Hah!!"

And the instant I involuntarily let out a shout like the temple-born guy, a Water Ball formed.

"Oh, wha...?"

Splash.

The instant I was startled, the Water Ball fell, just like that.

"...Ah"

Huh... I didn't chant just now, did I?

Why...?

All I'd done was copy the sensation from when I used the magic a moment ago, exactly as it was.

Could it be that if you can reproduce the flow of mana, you don't really have to chant?

Is chantless casting something you can do that easily?

Isn't that normally a high-tier skill?

"If it's that easy, then what's the point of an incantation?"

Even a beginner like me had been able to activate magic with chantless casting.

Gather the mana in my body at my fingertips, decide the shape in my head.

That's all it took.

Then there's no need for incantations. Everyone should just do it this way.

(...Hmm.)

Could it be that the incantation automates the magic for you?

Without having to concentrate each time and will the blood to gather from your whole body, just saying the words does it all for you.

Could that be all there is to it?

Like manual versus automatic on a car: maybe you can actually do it by hand, if you really want to?

'Chant the incantation, and it casts the magic for you automatically.'

This has big advantages.

First of all, it's easy to teach.

Rather than explaining, "It feels like you're gathering the blood from all the blood vessels in your body, aaand...," it's easier on teacher and student alike if anybody can do it in one go just by chanting.

And as it got taught that way, little by little, didn't it turn into 'the incantation is indispensable'?

Second, it's easy to use.

It goes without saying, but attack magic gets used in the middle of combat.

In combat, rattling off an incantation is quicker than shutting your eyes and concentrating, going "Nnnngh."

It's a question of which is easier: drawing an intricate picture while running at a full sprint, or saying a tongue twister while running at a full sprint.

"For some people, the first one might be easier, though..."

I flipped through the Magic Textbook, but there was nothing written about chantless casting.

Strange. From how it felt when I did it, it wasn't that hard.

Maybe I have some special talent, but it's not like nobody else could use it at all.

How about thinking of it this way.

Normally, mages, from beginners to experts, all keep using magic by incantation.

Over thousands, tens of thousands of uses, their bodies get completely used to the incantation.

So even when they do try to go chantless, they don't know how.

Hence it's considered not the norm, and it isn't written in the textbook.

"Oh, that adds up!!"

Which means that right now, I'm not the norm.

Isn't that freaking awesome?

Doesn't it totally feel like I just pulled off some secret cheat?

'IT CAN'T BE! THE CATALYST OF CLIMB, WITHOUT ORATORIO?'

'I ONLY USED THIS CATALYST THE NORMAL WAY TO OPEN THE CHANNEL, THOUGH.'

That's the vibe, right?

Wooo, I'm getting so hyped!!

Whoa, whoa, no good, no good. Chill out a sec. Be cool.

When I was alive, this exact feeling fooled me, and that's how I ended up like that.

Being better with computers than most people gave me a chosen-one complex, so I got cocky and blew it.

Tone it down, tone it down. What matters is not thinking I'm above other people.

I'm a beginner. A beginner.

Just a bowling newbie who lucked into a strike on his very first throw.

Beginner's luck. No kidding myself that I've got talent or whatever; I should just grind away at practice.

Okay. First I chant the spell's incantation aloud, then copy that feeling and practice chantless, nonstop.

Let's go with that.

"All right then, one more shot."

And when I put my right hand out, it felt weirdly sluggish.

But, it felt as if something heavy was sitting squarely on my shoulders.

Fatigue.

Because I'd been concentrating?

No, I'm an online-game pro (self-proclaimed), if a small-time one: a man who, when the situation called for it, could hunt for six days straight without sleep.

My concentration shouldn't be the kind that snaps after this little.

"Which means... I'm out of MP...?"

Oh, come on... If total mana is fixed at birth, then my mana comes to two Water Balls' worth.

Isn't that way too little? Or is it one of those deals where I'm wasting mana because it's my first time?

No, that's ridiculous.

Today I did them all chantless, so whether I chant or not shouldn't be what changed it.

Beginner magic, just as the word says, sits at the lowest rank of attack magic.

Among those, Water Ball and Fire Bullet are Beginner spells positioned as especially introductory.

'Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Saint, King, Emperor, God'

Two months later.

After a lot of trial and error, I was finally able to make a Water Ball fly.

That was the breakthrough, and I more or less figured out how incantations work.

An incantation follows a set process.

Generation → size setting → launch speed setting → activation.

Of these, the spell is completed by the caster setting the size and the launch speed themselves.

In other words, when you chant an incantation.

1. First, the form of the magic you want to use is created automatically.

2. After that, within a set time, you add mana and adjust the size.

3. After adjusting the size, by adding more mana within a further set time, you adjust the launch speed.

4. When the preparation time ends, it leaves the caster's hand and fires automatically.

And that's how it goes.

Probably not wrong... I think.

The trick was adding mana in two separate goes after the incantation.

If you don't adjust the size, you never get to adjusting the launch speed.

No wonder that when I tried to make it fly, it only got bigger and nothing happened.

Incidentally, when you do it chantless, you have to do all of those processes yourself.

That seems like a hassle, but you can cut down the input wait time for size and launch speed.

It lets you fire far faster than chanting.

Also, chantless, I could tinker with the generation part too.

For example, though it isn't in the textbook, freezing a Water Ball to make an Ice Bullet.

If I keep practicing this, I'll probably be able to pull off stuff like Kaiser Phoenix (smug face).

Depending on your ideas, the applications are endless.

This is getting fun!!

'Raise my total mana'

'Become able to use magic chantless as easily as breathing'

These two are my next tasks.

Because if you set a big goal right off the bat, you'll give up halfway.

Start small and keep chipping away.

Allll right, let's do this.
