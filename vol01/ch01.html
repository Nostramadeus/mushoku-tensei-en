Episode 1: Did You Mean: Another World

When I woke up, the first thing I felt was the glare.

Light spread across my whole field of vision, and I narrowed my eyes in discomfort.

As my eyes gradually adjusted, I could tell that a young blonde woman was peering down at me.

A beautiful girl... no, I'd call her a beautiful woman.

(Who's this?)

Beside her was a brown-haired man, also still young, turning an awkward smile on me.

A strong-looking, selfish-looking guy. The muscles on him were insane.

Brown hair, selfish-looking, all that... a DQN-ish guy like this should set off an allergic reaction in me on sight, but strangely, I felt no disgust.

Probably, it was because his hair wasn't dyed. It was a pretty brown.

"──××──××××"

The woman looked at me, smiled brightly, and said something.

What is she saying, I wonder. It's somehow muffled and hard to make out, and I don't understand it at all.

Could it be that it's not Japanese?

"────×××××───×××..."

The man, too, answered with a slack, easy face. No, really, I have no idea what they're saying.

"──××──×××"

From somewhere, I heard a third voice.

I couldn't see them.

I tried to sit up and ask where this was, and who they might be.

I may have been a shut-in, but it's not like I'm socially crippled.

I can manage that much.

"Aah, uwaah."

Or so I meant to, but what came out of my mouth was a sound I couldn't tell apart as either a groan or a gasp.

My body wouldn't move, either.

I could feel my fingertips and arms moving, but I couldn't lift my upper body.

"×××───××××××"

And, just as I thought that, the man picked me up in his arms.

Seriously? Over 100 kilos of me, and he picks me up this easily...

No, if I'd been bedridden for dozens of days, I'd have lost weight, I guess.

It was that bad an accident. Odds are good I'm missing an arm or a leg, too.

(What a living hell...)

That day.

That was the kind of thing I was thinking.

A month went by.

Apparently, I had been reborn. I had finally managed to swallow that fact.

I was a baby.

Being picked up, having my head supported, and getting my own body into view was how I finally confirmed it.

I don't know why my memories of my previous life stuck around, but it's not like them sticking around is a problem.

Reincarnation with your memories intact—everybody's had that fantasy at least once.

Not that I ever thought that fantasy would come true for real...

The man and woman I saw first after I woke up were, it seemed, my parents.

As for their age, early twenties or so, I'd guess.

Clearly younger than I was in my previous life.

From where I stood at thirty-four, you could even call them youngsters.

Making a kid at that age. I'm downright jealous.

I'd noticed it from the first day, but apparently this wasn't Japan.

The language was different, my parents' features weren't Japanese, and their clothes looked somehow like folk costumes.

There was nothing that looked like a home appliance, either (a person in a maid outfit was cleaning with a rag), and the dishes, the furniture and so on were crude wooden things. Probably not a developed country.

The lighting, too, wasn't light bulbs; they used candles and lamps.

Then again, it's also possible that they're especially poor and can't even pay the electric bill.

...Could it be that that possibility is the likely one?

Since there was someone who looked like a maid, I'd assumed they must have a fair amount of money, but,

if I think of her as a sister of my father's or my mother's, nothing about it is strange. She'd at least clean the house.

Sure, I wanted to start over, but if I've been born into a family too poor to even pay the electric bill, the future's looking grim.

★　★　★

Another half year went by.

After half a year of listening to my parents talk, I'd come to understand the language reasonably well.

My grades in English hadn't been very good, but it seems to be true after all that being buried in your own native language slows down how fast you pick up another one. Or is this body's brain just well made, I wonder. Maybe because I'm still so young, I feel like I learn things unusually fast.

By this point, I could at least crawl.

Being able to move around is a wonderful thing.

I had never been so grateful that my body could move.

"The second I take my eyes off him, he's off somewhere."

"Nothing wrong with lively, is there? Right after he was born, he didn't cry at all, and that had us worried."

"He still never cries, does he."

Watching me move around, my parents said things like that.

I'm way past the age of bawling my head off just because I'm a little hungry.

Though, when it comes to things down below, I'll leak sooner or later even if I hold it in, so I take the liberty of letting it rip.

Crawling or not, once I could move around, I started to figure out all sorts of things.

First, this house: well-off.

The building was a two-story wooden structure, with five or more rooms. They employed one maid.

At first I'd thought the maid might be my aunt, but her manner toward my father and mother was deferential, so she probably wasn't family.

The location: the countryside.

The view from the window was an idyllic rural landscape.

Other houses were few and far between; in the wheat fields that covered everything, only two or three were visible.

Pretty deep in the countryside. No utility poles or streetlights or the like in sight. Maybe there's no power plant nearby.

I've heard that in other countries they bury the power lines underground, but in that case, it's strange that this house doesn't use electricity.

This is just way too rural. For a man like me, seasoned by the waves of civilization, it might be a bit much.

Even reborn, I want to get my hands on a computer, at least.

That was what I thought, until early one afternoon.

With nothing to do, I thought I'd look at the idyllic rural landscape or something, and, as usual, I clambered up onto a chair, looked out the window, and got a jolt.

Because my father was swinging a sword around in the yard.

(Hold on, huh? What's he doing?)

So my old man's the type who goes swinging stuff like that around at his age? So he's chuunibyou?

(Ah, crap...)

In my surprise, I slipped off the chair.

My undeveloped hands grabbed the chair but couldn't hold up my body, and I went falling toward the floor, the heavy back of my head first.

"Eek!!"

The instant I landed with a thud, I heard a scream.

When I looked, my mother had dropped the laundry and, with a hand over her mouth, was looking down at me, white as a sheet.

"Rudy!! Are you okay!?"

My mother rushed over in a panic and picked me up.

When our eyes met, her face filled with relief, and she let out a long breath.

"...Phew, you look okay."

(When someone's hit their head, you'd best not move them around too much, lady.)

I graciously warned her, in my head.

Judging by how panicked she was, I must have fallen in a dangerous way.

I went in back of the head first, too, so I might've turned into an idiot. Not that it'd change much.

My head throbbed. I had at least tried to hang on to the chair, and there hadn't been much force behind the fall.

Seeing that my mother wasn't all that panicked, it looked like I wasn't bleeding. Probably just a bump.

My mother was examining my head carefully.

Her expression all but said that a wound, if there was one, would be a disaster.

And finally, she put her hand on my head, and,

"Just in case... Divine power is rich nourishment; to that one whose strength is lost shall it give the strength to rise again. 'Healing'"

I almost burst out laughing.

Come on, so this is this country's "pain, pain, fly away"?

Or, following my sword-swinging father, is my mother chuunibyou too?

A warrior and a priest, and they got married, is that it?

So I thought, for all of a moment.

The moment my mother's hand seemed to glow faintly, the pain vanished in an instant.

(...Huh?)

"There, you're all right now. Believe it or not, Mommy used to be a well-known adventurer, you know."

My mother, saying it with pride.

I was confused.

Sword, warrior, adventurer, Healing, incantation, priest. Words like those went round and round inside me.

What was that, just now? What did she do?

"What's wrong?"

Hearing my mother's scream, my father poked his head in from outside the window.

He was sweating, maybe from swinging the sword around.

"Listen, dear, Rudy went and climbed up onto a chair, of all things... he nearly got badly hurt."

"Ah, a boy has to be at least that lively."

My slightly hysterical mother, and my father, breezily letting it slide.

A familiar sight.

But, this time, probably because I'd fallen on the back of my head, my mother didn't back down.

"Now listen, dear, this child has not even been alive a year yet. Worry about him more!!"

"You can say that, but. Kids get sturdy by falling and tumbling, don't they? Besides, if he gets hurt, you can just heal him every time."

"But when I think, what if he gets hurt too badly and I can't heal him, I get so worried..."

"It'll be fine."

Saying that, my father hugged my mother and me together.

My mother's face flushed red.

"At first he didn't cry, and that worried me, but if he's this much of a little rascal, he'll be fine..."

My father gave my mother a little smooch.

Oho, showing off for us, huh, you two? Wheet-whoo.

After that, the two of them put me to bed in the next room, then went upstairs and got down to the work of making me a little brother or sister.

Even when you go to the second floor, I can hear the creak creak, moan moan, so I know, you damn normies with a life...

(Still, magic, huh...)

★　★　★

From then on, I started listening carefully to the conversations of my parents and the help.

Then I noticed that there were a lot of words I'd never heard before.

Especially the names of countries, the names of territories, the names of regions. The proper nouns were all ones I'd never heard of.

Could it be that this place is...

No, I think I can say it for certain by now.

This isn't Earth; it's a different world.

It's another world of swords and magic.

Then, a thought struck me.

...That in this world, maybe I could do it too?

In a world of swords and magic, in a world whose common sense is different from when I was alive, couldn't even I do it?

Living like other people do, making an effort like other people do. Getting back up even when I stumble, and going on living, still facing forward.

When I was alive, I spent my dying moments in regret.

Holding on to my own powerlessness, and my frustration at never having done anything, I died.

But, if it's the me who knows that.

If it's the me of now, with the knowledge and experience from when I was alive, couldn't I do it?

—Living seriously this time.
