Episode 5: Swordsmanship and Magic

I turned five.

On my birthday, there was a modest little party.

In this country, apparently, there's no custom of celebrating your birthday every year. But, once you reach certain ages, it's customary for your family to give you something, I'm told.

Those certain ages: five, ten and fifteen.

You become an adult at fifteen, so it's all very easy to keep track of.

As a present, Paulo gave me a sword.

Two, actually.

A real sword, long and heavy for a five-year-old to carry, and a shortish wooden sword.

The real sword had been properly forged, and it had a sharp edge, too.

Not something a child has any business holding.

"A man must keep a single sword in his heart. If he is to protect those dear to him—"

This edifying lecture went on and on, so I smiled sweetly and let it go in one ear and out the other.

Paulo was holding forth in high spirits, but in the end, Zenith chided him: "Too long."

Paulo gave a wry smile and wrapped up with, "Accordingly, keep it put away except when it's needed."

Probably, what Paulo wanted to give me was an awareness of what it means to carry a sword, and the resolve that goes with it.

From Zenith, I got a book.

"Because Rudy loves books."

With that, she handed me a plant encyclopedia.

Without meaning to, I let out an "Ooh."

In this world, books are expensive. They seem to have papermaking but no printing, so it's all written by hand.

The plant encyclopedia was thick, and it explained everything carefully, with illustrations to make it easy to follow.

Who knows how much a thing like this even goes for.

"Thank you very much, Mother. This is just the kind of thing I wanted."

When I said that, I got squeezed in a tight hug.

From Roxy, I got a rod.

A stick about 30 cm long with a small red stone on the tip, a simple thing.

"I made it the other day. You were using magic from the very beginning, Rudy, so it slipped my mind, but a master is supposed to make a staff for a pupil who can use Beginner magic. I apologize."

Apparently that's how it's done.

Roxy had hated being called Master, but it seems ignoring the custom didn't sit right with her.

"Yes, Master. I will treasure it."

When I said that, Roxy gave a wry smile.

Starting the next day, my sword training began in earnest.

Basically, centered on practice swings and forms.

Checking my forms and how my strikes landed against a wooden dummy set up in the yard, or trading blows with my father to train my footwork and weight shifting: that sort of thing.

Very back-to-basics. Truly excellent.

In this world, swordsmanship is held in pretty high regard.

The heroes who show up in books, too, are almost all armed with swords. Once in a while someone carries an axe or a hammer, but they're the minority.

Nobody carries a spear, and that's because that notorious, hated bunch, the Superd tribe, used three-pronged spears. The spear is the devil's weapon. That's just common sense here. For what it's worth, a few of those devils did show up in the books, too. Cast as something like indiscriminate serial killers who devour friend and foe alike.

Maybe because of that background, the swordsmanship here outclasses that of the world I came from.

A master can cut a rock clean in two with one stroke, or send a sword flash flying to strike an opponent far away.

Case in point: Paulo can cut a mere rock in half.

I wanted to know how it worked, so I praised him, lauded him and buttered him up, and he demonstrated it for me again and again. His son, who can wield even Advanced magic at a tender age, clapping with delight: Paulo must have felt on top of the world.

Not that I ever figured out how it worked, however many times I watched.

Since watching didn't help, I tried asking him to explain, but...

"You zip in on the step and go SHING!! Like that."

"Like this!?"

"Idiot! That's heave in on the step and go THUD!! It's zip in on the step and go SHING! Lighter on your feet!"

That's how it went.

This is a guess, but swordsmanship in this world has mana mixed up in it.

Unlike magic, which shows up looking every bit as magical as you'd picture, swordsmanship specializes in things like strengthening the body and strengthening the metal of the sword and so on. Otherwise, how could anyone tear around at super-high speed and cut rocks in half?

Granted, Paulo isn't aware he's using mana.

Hence, he can't explain it, either.

But, if I can learn to reproduce it, it'll be as good as being able to use body-strengthening boost magic.

Time to put in the work.

★　★　★

In this world, there are three mainstream schools.

—One is the Sword God Style.

An aggressive swordsmanship that all but says offense is the best defense, a school that puts speed first, as if its whole purpose were to land your sword on the opponent first, no matter what.

Seize sen no sen, and kill with one blow.

If that doesn't finish them, keep up the hit-and-run until it does.

If I had to map it onto my original world, it'd be something like Satsuma Jigen-ryu.

—One is the Water God Style.

This one is the polar opposite of the Sword God Style.

A defensive swordsmanship built around parrying and counters.

Its motto is exclusively defensive defense, so it has few moves for going out and attacking.

But a master, apparently, can unleash a counter against any attack at all.

Any attack—even magic and projectiles.

It's the sword learned by the likes of court knights and nobles, people whose role centers on protecting.

—One is the North God Style.

This one seems to be less swordsmanship than the art of war.

It has no signature techniques; its selling point is supposedly flexibility, adapting to whatever the situation calls for.

According to Paulo, for all the talk of flexibility, it mostly comes down to cheap, sneaky little tricks.

But, master it, and it becomes truly outlandish.

Something like a swordsmanship version of Jack○e Chan, apparently.

Since it's a school you can fight with even while nursing wounds or missing a body part, it is, at least, the swordsmanship favored by mercenaries, adventurers and that sort.

These are called the three great styles, and they have practitioners all over the world.

Those who want to reach the absolute limit as swordsmen knock on the door of one school or another and, apparently, keep swinging a sword until they die.

But, such people are few.

If you want to get reasonably strong fast, the standard approach, apparently, is to dabble in a few schools and cherry-pick the good parts.

In fact, Paulo too mainly uses the Sword God Style while dabbling in both the Water God Style and the North God Style.

Sword God or Water God, either one is probably too peaky a style to go out into the world with on its own.

Incidentally, these sword styles are also divided into ranks, as follows.

Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Saint, King, Emperor, God.

Also, each school has "God" in its name because of what its founder was popularly called.

The first swordsman of the Water God Style was, reportedly, at the same time a mage who could wield Water God-rank magic.

God rank with the sword, God rank with magic: so naturally, that swordsman was off-the-charts, stupidly strong, apparently.

Incidentally, when you're naming a swordsman you say 'Water God' or 'Water Saint,' but when you're naming a mage, it's the norm to tack on 'rank,' as in 'Water God-rank' or 'Water Saint-rank,' I'm told.

For example, Roxy is a 'Water Saint-rank mage.'

★　★　★

It was decided I'd learn two of them: the Sword God Style and the Water God Style.

Sword God for offense, Water God for defense. That's the idea.

"But, Father. From what you have told me, it seems to me that the North God Style is the best balanced of them."

"Don't talk stupid. That's just fighting with a sword in your hand. It's not swordsmanship."

"I see."

Of the three schools, the North God Style seems to get discriminated against.

Or maybe it's just that Paulo personally hates it.

For someone who hates it, Paulo's apparently Advanced in the North God Style too, though.

"Rudy, you've got a gift for magic, looks like, but it won't hurt you any to learn the sword. Be the kind of mage who can weather a Sword God Style slash."

"A magic swordsman... is that what you mean?"

"Hm? A magic swordsman is a swordsman who can use magic. With you it's the other way around, isn't it?"

What's the difference supposed to be?

Whether you class-change from Warrior or class-change from Wizard, a magic swordsman is a magic swordsman, I'd think.

Either way, if I train my swordsmanship, I can apply it to magic, too.

The problem is that Paulo does the body-strengthening boost unconsciously, so he's no help teaching it.

I'll have to pick it up on my own somehow, but can you really get there just by training your body?

Gotta get to the bottom of how it works, one way or another...

"...So you don't like the sword after all?"

While I was lost in thought, Paulo asked me that, looking anxious.

Maybe because of all this talk about me having a talent for magic.

Paulo seemed to be worried that I didn't want sword lessons.

But don't get the wrong idea. It's not that I hate sword lessons. It's just that, next to working up a nice, refreshing sweat in the yard with some grubby, musky dude, I'd rather have my little study sessions with Roxy, just the two of us.

I'm the indoor type.

Granted, that's a matter of likes and dislikes.

Since I've decided to live seriously in this world, I'll put in the work on both the sword and magic, just watch me.

"No. I would like to become as good with the sword as I am with magic."

Paulo looked all choked up at that; he nodded happily and took up his wooden sword.

"All right, then, let's start on strikes. Come at me!!"

A simple man.

Magic and swordsmanship. Which one I'll end up relying on in the end, I don't know.

Not gonna lie, I don't care which.

"Yes!! Father!!"

But being a good son is something you should start on early.

When I was alive, I gave my parents nothing but grief until the day they died.

If I'd been kinder to my parents, maybe my siblings wouldn't have pulled a stunt like suddenly throwing me out of the house.

So, I've got to take good care of my parents.

★　★　★

So, around the time I took my first steps into swordsmanship, my magic lessons, for their part, had moved on to fairly technical and practical territory.

"If you produce Waterfallwater cascade, Heat Islandearth heat and Icicle Fieldfreezing zone in that order, what happens?"

"Fog forms."

"Correct. Then, to clear that fog?"

"Um, you use Heat Islandearth heat again and warm the ground."

"That is exactly right. Please show me."

Using multiple elements in sequence to produce a phenomenon.

This is called 'combined magic.'

The Magic Textbook has magic for making rain fall, but for some reason, no spell for making fog.

So mages use magic of different elements in sequence. That way, they reproduce natural phenomena.

This world, with no microscopes.

They probably haven't gotten as far as working out the principles behind natural phenomena.

Combined magic is packed with the ingenuity of the mages of old.

Not that I need to bother with anything that tedious.

I can just take the magic that makes a cloud and brings rain, and cast it right down at ground level.

But, the idea of deliberately producing natural phenomena is easy to grasp.

If I rack my brain, it seems like I could do all sorts of things.

A bit much for my brain, though.

"Magic can do anything, can't it?"

"It cannot do everything. You must not overestimate it. Simply stay calm, and steadily carry out what you are able to do, and what you ought to do."

So Roxy chided me, but inside my head, words like 'railgun' and 'optical camouflage' were dancing around.

"Besides, if you go around boasting that you can do anything, you will be saddled with the things you cannot do as well."

"Is that from experience, Sensei?"

"It is."

I see. I'll have to watch out for that.

Getting stuck with stuff is a pain, after all.

"But, are there really people who push that much work onto mages?"

"Yes. Advanced-rank mages are not all that numerous, you see."

One person in twenty can fight.

Of those, a further one in twenty is a mage.

That's roughly how it goes, apparently.

So mages come to about one in four hundred.

Mages in themselves aren't all that rare, but,

"Someone who studied properly all the way to graduating from a magic school... In other words, Advanced-rank mages come to about one in a hundred mages, I would say."

Advanced-rank mages: one in forty thousand.

If you can handle combined magic on top of Intermediate and Advanced magic, what you can do goes up by leaps and bounds.

Which is why, apparently, everybody wants a piece of them.

Home tutors in this country, too, need to be qualified at Advanced or higher.

It carries a lot of weight as a credential, too.

"There are such things as magic schools?"

"Yes. Any major country has magic schools."

Still, I'd figured there might be, but a magic school, huh.

Is it about to kick off? The school arc.

"But, the biggest of them all, as you might expect, would be the Ranoa University of Magic."

Oho, there's a university too?

"How is that university different from the other schools?"

"It has good facilities and good teachers, all in one place. You can take lectures there more modern and advanced than what you would learn at other schools."

"Did you go to the university too, Sensei?"

"I did. Although, since the magic schools are very prestigious, a demon like me could only get into the University of Magic..."

The magic schools of the Kingdom of Ranoa, the kind the sons of nobles attend, turn you away at screening just for not being human, apparently.

Discrimination against demons is dying down, but they still catch a lot of flak, apparently.

"The Ranoa University of Magic has no strange airs or pride. A correct theory will not be brushed off, however eccentric it is, and because it accepts all kinds of races, research into each race's own magic is advancing as well. If you wish to follow the path of magic, Rudy, I recommend the path that leads to the University of Magic."

Probably partly because it's her alma mater, but she's praising it to the skies.

Anyway, that's a ways off yet.

Plus, if I enrolled at five, I might get picked on and stuff.

"I think it may still be too early to decide that sort of thing..."

"You are right. I think it would also be fine to follow Paulo-sama's wishes and take the path of a swordsman or a knight. There have been people who earned the title of knight and then went abroad to study at the University of Magic. Please do not think there is only one path, the sword or magic, one or the other. There is also the path of the magic swordsman."

"Yes."

Still.

Just the opposite of Paulo, Roxy seemed to be worried that I might hate magic.

Lately my mana has grown, and I've gotten a handle on the rules, too.

So I've ended up sitting through more and more lessons with my mind somewhere else.

Magic lessons forced on me at age three to begin with.

Over these two years, I've gotten sick of them.

Maybe that's what she figured.

Paulo, seeing my talent for magic.

Roxy, seeing how keen I was on the sword.

Each for a different reason, they were probably showing me that there's a middle path, too.

"But that's still a long way off, isn't it?"

"For you, Rudy, it is."

Roxy gave a lonely smile.

"However, there is not much left that I can teach you. Graduation is not far off, either, so it should be all right to talk about this sort of thing."

...Graduation?
